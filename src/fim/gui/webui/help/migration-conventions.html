Who this document is for

Anyone comparing fim's own output — a simulated run, or the exact gene-identity recursion implemented in test_simulator_equilibrium.py — against a published population-genetics paper. Every comparison of this kind needs a mapping between two independently chosen sets of conventions before the numbers mean the same thing; get the mapping wrong, or skip it, and an entirely correct simulator and an entirely correct paper can disagree substantially while both sides are right (§2.1). This document derives that mapping once rather than leaving it implicit in each test — the arrangement that let the Crow & Aoki discrepancy go unexplained for as long as it did.

The aim is not to make a published number match at any cost. First translate the paper's definitions into the simulator's definitions. Then compare the resulting recursions or statistics. If a discrepancy remains, report it as unresolved rather than tuning parameters until the desired number appears.

Notation and comparison procedure

Use one locus and a fixed generation while making each comparison. Let d be the number of demes, Nk the number of individuals in deme k, c the number of gene copies per individual, and nk the number of copies at that locus:

nk=cNk(M.1)

For haploids, c=1; for diploids, c=2. This conversion is made before using any formula stated in gene copies. For example, 100 diploid individuals have n=200 gene copies at one autosomal locus. Tracking another locus does not double n.

Write pk(a) for the frequency of allele a in deme k. The with-replacement within-deme identity is the probability that two independent draws from that frequency distribution match:

Jkwr=∑apk(a)2(M.2)

The wr superscript means with replacement: the two draws may select the same physical copy. Write Jkdist for the probability that two distinct copies match, sampled without replacement. These are both valid identity probabilities, but they are not interchangeable at finite population size.

For each published comparison, check the assumptions in this order:

  1. Convert individuals to gene copies, if needed.
  2. Match which demes and copy fractions contribute to a destination's parental pool.
  3. Match whether identity draws permit selecting one physical copy twice.
  4. Align the generation boundary and mutation step.
  5. Only then compare recursions, trajectories, or statistics.

Changing only one convention at a time makes each correction auditable. A fit at one equilibrium point is weaker evidence than agreement across a trajectory, because distinct recursions can share one fixed point.

1. fim's four conventions

Four independent choices distinguish the fim conventions from those used in some cited papers. Each convention formalizes the same biological process under different assumptions or definitions.

1.1 Population unit

The configuration key N accepts individual counts and ploidy; the engine uses nk=cNk gene copies per deme and locus. See the configuration reference. A paper that gives an individual count must be converted before substituting its value into a gene-copy equation. This factor is not an extra factor to apply when the paper already defines its N as a copy count.

For example, a paper's 100 diploid individuals and fim's N: 200, ploidy: haploid represent the same 200 copies at a locus. By contrast, N: 100, ploidy: diploid also represents 200 copies. Comparing the bare numbers 100 and 200 without their units would make the drift terms differ by a factor of two.

1.2 Migration source

In the equal-size scalar island model, fim retains a fraction 1−mfim locally and distributes its incoming fraction equally among the other d−1 demes:

Mkjfim={1−mfim,j=k,mfim/(d−1),j≠k.(M.3)

Mkj is the share of destination k's parental pool supplied by source deme j. Every row sums to one: the diagonal supplies 1−mfim, and the other d−1 entries sum to mfim.

Some papers instead designate fraction mpaper for redrawing from a pool of all d demes, including home:

Mkjpaper=(1−mpaper)1{j=k}+mpaperd(M.4)

The indicator 1{j=k} is one for the home source and zero otherwise. The home weight is therefore 1−mpaper+mpaper/d; every other source has weight mpaper/d. Equate an off-diagonal source weight in (M.3) and (M.4):

mfimd−1=mpaperd(M.5)

Solving for either rate gives the conversion:

mpaper=mfimdd−1,mfim=mpaperd−1d(M.6)

This conversion equates the complete matrix row, not just the word "migration." For d=4 and mfim=0.06, the paper's equivalent redraw rate is 0.08. The paper's home weight is 0.92 and each other weight 0.02; fim's home weight is 0.94 and each of three other weights is 0.02. The destination-1 row in each matrix is (0.94,0.02,0.02,0.02).

For unequal deme sizes, fim's scalar all-other pool is copy-size-weighted; for a general matrix, each row already gives the full weights. Do not apply (M.6) to a spatial topology merely because its migration parameter has the same name. See the migration definition in the design and configuration reference.

1.3 Identity sampling

Suppose a deme contains exactly n copies and its observed frequency of allele a is p(a). Two independent draws with replacement match with probability:

Jwr=∑ap(a)2(M.7)

There are n2 ordered pairs of copy positions if repetition is allowed. Of those, n are self-pairs and always match. The remaining n(n−1) are ordered distinct-copy pairs; their matching fraction is Jdist. Thus:

Jwr=1n+(1−1n)Jdist(M.8)

The first term is the probability of selecting the same physical copy twice. The coefficient of Jdist is the chance of selecting different copies. Solve for the paper's distinct-pair identity:

Jdist=nJwr−1n−1,n>1(M.9)

For counts 7 and 3 among ten copies, Jwr=(7/10)2+(3/10)2=0.58. There are seven self-pairs, plus 7·6+3·2=48 matching ordered distinct pairs among 10·9=90: Jdist=48/90=0.5333…. Equation (M.9) gives the same result: (10·0.58−1)/9=0.5333….

Between-deme draws already refer to distinct physical copies because they come from different demes, so their identity probability requires no same-deme correction. When n=1, there are no distinct pairs within the deme and (M.9) is undefined; do not divide by zero or invent a value for that sampling question.

1.4 Operator order and generation boundaries

fim advances a generation as Migrate → Drift → Mutate: migration changes parental frequencies, drift draws whole copies, and mutation independently replaces newly drawn labels. The design document gives the generation pipeline. Ryman & Leimar write their corresponding cycle as Drift → Migrate → Mutate. A different written starting point does not automatically mean a different model: for a deterministic linear migration map and the paired identity state, the two cycle descriptions can be aligned by translating the state at the generation boundary.

The relevant state translation is (M.8) for within-deme identity. Between-deme identity is already a distinct-copy probability. With the migration-rate conversion (M.6), the comparison in §2.1 shows the resulting recursions agree generation by generation. Do not add an arbitrary one-generation lag after applying this translation; that would compare different biological phases. This equivalence is established for the specific symmetric, equal-size, infinite-alleles recursions under discussion, not for any possible pair of operators that happen to be reordered.

2. Per-paper mappings

2.1 Ryman & Leimar (2008)

Their island model uses s for the number of islands, N for the number of diploid individuals per island, 2N gene copies per locus, mpaper for migration from a pool that includes home, and u for the per-copy infinite-alleles mutation probability. To compare against fim, use d=s and the gene-copy count:

n=2N(M.10)

This is a unit conversion, not an extra factor on a value that already counts copies. The table and derivations below use n for fim's per-deme gene-copy count to avoid overloading the paper's individual-count symbol N.

Match the migration coefficients

For the paper's self-inclusive redraw, a lineage in island i has source probabilities $1-m_{\mathrm{paper}}+ m_{\mathrm{paper}}/sforsourcei$, and mpaper/s for each other source. For two copies starting in that same island, the probability their ancestral sources coincide is:

a=(1−m+ms)2+(s−1)(ms)2=(1−m)2+m(2−m)s(M.11)

Here m=mpaper. The first squared term is the chance both trace to the home island. The remaining s−1 squared terms cover their matching on any one of the other islands. Expanding and collecting the 1/s terms gives the rightmost form.

For two copies starting in different islands i and j, there are two ways for both ancestral sources to be i or j, plus the s−2 other common sources:

b=2(1−m+ms)ms+(s−2)(ms)2=m(2−m)s(M.12)

The rightmost equality follows by expanding the first term: $2m(1-m)/s+2m^2/s^2+(s-2)m^2/s^2 =2m(1-m)/s+m^2/s$. Thus a is a same-island source-match probability and b is a different-island source-match probability.

Set s=d and convert m with (M.6). For d=4 and mfim=0.06, the paper uses m=0.08. Equation (M.12) gives b=0.08(1.92)/4=0.0384; (M.11) gives a=0.922+0.0384=0.8848. These coefficients describe pair ancestry under the paper's migration convention; they are not themselves allele frequencies.

Translate identity and write the paper's recursion

Let J0 be distinct-copy identity within an island and J1 identity between islands. Drift in one deme first changes the within identity to:

J~0=1n+(1−1n)J0(M.13)

The probability 1/n of sampling the same parental copy creates the first term. If two copies are distinct, probability 1−1/n, their identity is the previous distinct-pair value. Between-island identity has no same-deme self-pair term.

Pairwise migration then mixes the same-island and different-island cases using a and b. Finally, both copies must avoid independent mutation to remain identical. With α=(1−u)2:

J0′=α[aJ~0+(1−a)J1](M.14)
J1′=α[bJ~0+(1−b)J1](M.15)

For J0′, migration traces both copies to the same island with probability a, where their match probability is J~0; otherwise they trace to different islands with identity J1. Equation (M.15) uses probability b for the corresponding same-island ancestry when the current demes are different. In both cases, mutation preserves the old label only if both copies avoid mutation.

fim stores Jwr within each deme. Convert it to the paper's distinct-copy state with (M.9), while retaining between-deme identity:

J0=Jdist=nJwr−1n−1,J1=Jbetweenwr(M.16)

For n=10 and Jwr=0.58, this gives J0=0.5333…. Then apply (M.13)–(M.15) with the mapped migration rate. This state translation, together with (M.6), is why the different written cycle boundaries do not require an extra generation of lag in the tested comparison.

Once the identity convention is aligned, calculate each differentiation statistic from the corresponding within and between identities using the same paper formulas. Do not confuse J0 or J1 with GST: they are identity inputs to the statistics, not differentiation statistics themselves. Because between-deme draws are already distinct, no conversion is applied to J1.

Numerical verification and size of the corrections

The mapping is checked by comparing the paper's Eqs. 2–3 as transcribed in test_simulator_equilibrium.py against fim's exact identity recursion. The test grid includes n∈{200,2000,20000}, d∈{2,5,10,50}, migration rates in {0,10−4,10−3,10−2,10−1}, and mutation rates in {0,10−6,10−4,10−3}. The test source records a broader 240-combination analysis, sampled at 714 generations across each 5000-generation trajectory; the committed regression exercises representative parameter combinations at 1, 10, 100, 1000, and 5000 generations. Paper units and migration rates are mapped before comparison. Across the grid, differences in GST and D stay at floating-point accumulation scale under the shared (1−u)2 mutation rule.

Without those mappings, the broader comparison recorded up to 58% relative error; after mapping, the historical maximum residual was about 0.1%. The current regression does not rerun every point of that larger analysis.

An unmapped migration comparison can be large. At d=10, mpaper/mfim=10/9; at d=2 the ratio is 2. These rates are not numerically equal because one redraw distributes mass across d sources and the other only across d−1 external sources.

The identity conversion is an O(1/n) correction to the with-replacement probability. When identity is near one early in a trajectory, even that small absolute correction can be large relative to a small differentiation statistic. The validation analysis recorded a 22% relative gap in GST at n=2000, d=10, u=10−4, generation 10, before conversion. This is why checking only a large-population equilibrium can conceal a convention mismatch that matters for trajectory comparisons.

The mutation update is also part of the comparison. For two distinct copies under infinite alleles, the shared-label survival factor is:

α=(1−u)2(M.17)

Either copy mutating makes its label new, so both must avoid mutation. A proportional update that includes the sampling correction adds the term u(1−u)/n and does not match the distinct-copy recursion.

2.2 Crow & Aoki (1984)

Unresolved. Crow & Aoki's Table 1 reports GST=0.172 for a two-dimensional stepping-stone (torus) scenario with n=9 demes, 20 individuals per deme, M=1.0 expected migrants, and mutation rate 10−5. The test uses the project's interpretation m=M/N=1/20=0.05, divided evenly among each deme's four neighbors. The exact identity recursion under that matrix gives GST≈0.324, without random sampling noise. The applicable source is test_simulator_equilibrium.py, test test_pairwise_identity_recursion_applied_to_the_crow_aoki_torus.

Applying a spatial analogue of the self-inclusive pool convention—distributing the pool across the home deme and its four neighbors—gives approximately 0.374, farther from the reported 0.172. This rejects that specific migration mapping as the explanation; it does not identify the paper's actual convention.

An exploratory rate near m=0.12 gives approximately 0.172, while m=0.2 gives approximately 0.115. This is a clue that the rate interpretation may matter, not evidence that 0.12 is the authors' intended value. The paper describes its stepping-stone values as unpublished numerical calculations and does not specify the exact division of migrants among the four neighbors. The discrepancy therefore remains unresolved. See the test plan's Appendix A for the broader record.

The island-model conversion (M.6) cannot be transplanted blindly to this topology. The island derivation assumes equal weighting of all d−1 other demes; a torus has only four local neighbors. One must specify the entire row of the spatial migration matrix, including whether the home deme participates, before comparing rates.

3. Comparison protocol

This reference makes the assumptions in each literature comparison inspectable and prevents equations from being compared under incompatible units or definitions. For each paper, list its variables and source-pool rule, derive any conversion, show the identity definition, state the operator phase, and record both positive and unresolved checks.

Numerical agreement is evidence only after the translation is derived independently of the target value. Pin deterministic recursion comparisons with fixed tolerances. For stochastic simulations, separately account for Monte Carlo uncertainty; do not use a seed search to force agreement.

The relevant implementation points are identity_recursion() and migrate(). The Python API guide and generated API reference document programmatic access; the statistics reference lists result identifiers.
