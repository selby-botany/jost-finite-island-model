Nei distances in fim: two denominators, pairs and all demes

fim reports Nei's genetic distance in eight forms, together with the corresponding genetic identities. This guide defines each form, explains why they can differ, and describes the conditions under which the all-demes geometric distance is negative.

Mathematical symbols use TEX; literal setting names, statistic keys, and code identifiers use backticks. Each displayed equation has one unique tag beginning with N for this document.

Who this is for

Written first for a researcher reading fim's results, with no programming assumed. The derivations and worked examples are for anyone who wants to check the arithmetic step by step. Statistic keys such as NEI_D_PAIR_ARITH are the literal names used in saved results and in the Settings dialog; they are not mathematical variables. The statistics reference lists statistic definitions and result identifiers.

The short version

  • Nei's distance compares how often two gene copies match between demes with how often they match within demes. Zero means the demes are alike; it grows as they diverge. It is infinite when the selected identity rule yields zero.
  • The denominator (the "within" part) can be a geometric mean (Nei's 1972 original) or an arithmetic mean (the version Jost uses). The arithmetic-denominator distance is never smaller; the two agree exactly when the demes are equally diverse.
  • Pair forms compare the two demes chosen for the scatter plot. All-demes forms summarize every deme at once.
  • With several loci, Nei's own rule combines loci first and takes one ratio (the default). The per-locus mean takes one distance per locus and averages them.
  • The all-demes geometric form can be negative. That is not an error; it means within-deme diversity is very uneven across demes. See When the distance is negative.
  • Every form is computed and saved for every run. Choose which ones to see in Settings, "Statistics shown"; the Nei family starts hidden.

What a Nei distance measures

At one locus, let pk,i be the frequency of allele i in deme k. If two copies are sampled independently from that deme, both copies carry allele i with probability pk,ipk,i. Add those non-overlapping allele cases to obtain the within-deme gene identity:

Jk=∑ipk,i2(N.1)

For example, if deme X has frequencies (0.75,0.25), then:

JX=0.752+0.252=0.5625+0.0625=0.625(N.2)

For one copy from deme X and one from deme Y, the probability that both are allele i is pX,ipY,i. Summing over the alleles present in either deme gives the between-deme identity:

JXY=∑ipX,ipY,i(N.3)

If Y has frequencies (0.25,0.75) over the same two alleles, the products are 0.75(0.25)=0.1875 for the first allele and 0.25(0.75)=0.1875 for the second. Therefore:

JXY=0.1875+0.1875=0.375(N.4)

The between-deme identity is smaller here because each deme favors a different allele. Nei's identity I compares JXY with a chosen average of the within-deme identities. Identical allele-frequency distributions give I=1; less between-deme matching gives a smaller identity.

The distance transforms identity with the negative natural logarithm:

D=−ln(I)(N.5)

Since −ln(1)=0, identical populations have distance zero. As I falls toward zero, the distance grows; at I=0, the limiting distance is infinite. Under Nei's mutation model, this logarithm makes the distance increase approximately in proportion to elapsed time after isolated populations separate.

The two denominators

"A typical within-deme identity" can be averaged two ways.

Form Pair denominator Source
Geometric JXJY Nei (1972)
Arithmetic (JX+JY)/2 Jost, L. (2026) private communication

For positive within identities, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality says that the arithmetic mean across d demes is at least the geometric mean. The pair case is d=2:

1d∑k=1dJk≥(∏k=1dJk)1/d(N.6)

For a pair, equality holds exactly when JX=JY; across all demes, it holds exactly when every Jk is equal. Both identities divide the same between-deme identity by these denominators, so:

Iarith≤Igeo(N.7)

For a pair, the cross-identity is an inner product of two frequency vectors. Cauchy-Schwarz bounds it by the product of their lengths:

JXY≤JXJY(N.6a)

Thus the pair geometric identity cannot exceed one; (N.7) bounds the pair arithmetic identity as well. Across all demes, Jkℓ≤(Jk+Jℓ)/2 for each pair, so averaging every pair also bounds the all-demes arithmetic numerator:

Jbetween≤1d∑k=1dJk(N.6b)

This proves that the all-demes arithmetic identity is at most one. Only the all-demes geometric identity lacks that bound.

Applying −ln(·) reverses that ordering:

  • the arithmetic identity is never larger than the geometric one, and the arithmetic distance is never smaller;
  • they agree exactly when the two demes are equally diverse (JX=JY), and differ most when one deme is much more diverse than the other.

Comparing the two is therefore itself informative: a large gap between them says the demes differ in diversity, not only in which alleles they carry. For the example above, JX=JY=0.625, so the denominators coincide even though the allele frequencies differ. The two forms then give the same identity: I=JXY/0.625=0.375/0.625=0.6. The distance is −ln(0.6)≈0.511. A denominator difference appears only when the within-deme identities differ.

A pair of demes, or all of them

Pair forms (NEI_D_PAIR_*) compare two demes: the pair selected in the scatter plot, or Demes 1 and 2 by default. Values update with the selected pair in the live view, in completed runs, and across generations. A deme compared with itself has distance 0.

All-demes forms (NEI_D_ALL_*) summarize every deme at once. At one locus, for d demes, the numerator averages Jkℓ over all unordered pairs; the denominator averages or geometrically combines the d within-deme identities:

Jbetween=2d(d−1)∑k<ℓJkℓ(N.8)
Iall,geo=Jbetween(∏k=1dJk)1/d(N.9)
Iall,arith=Jbetween1d∑k=1dJk(N.10)

For two demes, there is one unordered pair and the all-demes numerator is exactly J12. Its two within identities are J1 and J2, so each all-demes form equals its matching pair form.

Every run also saves the pair values for every pair of demes in pairwise.json, so a pair you did not look at during the run can still be compared later (see Where to find the values).

Several loci: two ways to combine them

Most runs track L loci, and the two rules for combining them can give different answers. Write Jk,ℓ and JXY,ℓ for the within- and between-deme identities at locus ℓ.

Nei's pooled rule averages each identity over loci first. For a pair, these pooled quantities are:

J―X=1L∑ℓ=1LJX,ℓ(N.11)
J―Y=1L∑ℓ=1LJY,ℓ(N.12)
J―XY=1L∑ℓ=1LJXY,ℓ(N.13)

Then fim applies the chosen denominator once to those three pooled identities and takes one logarithm:

Ipool,geo=J―XYJ―XJ―Y(N.14)
Ipool,arith=J―XY(J―X+J―Y)/2(N.15)

The same sequence applies to all demes. First average each within-deme identity and the between-deme identity across loci:

J―k=1L∑ℓ=1LJk,ℓ(N.16)
J―between=1L∑ℓ=1LJbetween,ℓ(N.17)

Then insert those averages in the all-demes ratios (N.9) and (N.10). A locus contributes according to how much identity it supplies; it is not assigned an independent, equal-weight distance.

Per-locus mean (the _LOCUS_MEAN forms) instead computes one identity and distance at each locus, then averages the distances. If Iℓ is the selected pair or all-demes identity for locus ℓ:

Dlocus mean=1L∑ℓ=1L−ln(Iℓ)(N.18)

Using ln(∏xℓ)=∑ln(xℓ) shows that the corresponding reported identity is the geometric mean of the per-locus identities:

Ilocus mean=exp(−Dlocus mean)=(∏ℓ=1LIℓ)1/L(N.19)

For example, if two loci have identities 0.8 and 0.2, their per-locus mean distance is [−ln(0.8)−ln(0.2)]/2≈0.9163, and the equivalent identity is 0.8(0.2)=0.4. This is not the arithmetic mean of the locus identities (which would be 0.5).

The two rules differ most when loci behave differently. If any single locus has zero identity, its distance is infinite, so the per-locus mean is infinite. The pooled rule can remain finite if other loci have positive between-deme identity: a zero at one locus does not make the average between-deme identity zero.

Cost: the per-locus mean reuses the identities Nei's rule already computes; it adds one ratio and one logarithm per locus, which is not measurable next to the rest of a run.

When the distance is infinite

For a pair, JXY,ℓ=0 when the demes share no allele at locus ℓ. Under the pooled rule, I=0 only if the mean J―XY is zero, which requires zero cross-identity at every locus. Under the per-locus mean, one locus with Iℓ=0 is enough: −ln(0) is infinite, and averaging that distance remains infinite. The same distinction applies to all-demes identities, where the per-locus numerator averages cross-identities over all deme pairs.

The screen shows ∞, with the tooltip "infinite (no allele is shared)". Strict JSON cannot represent infinity, so report.json stores null for the distance; the matching identity in NEI_I_... is finite and is zero in this case. A batch summary omits a distance that is infinite in any replicate, because the mean then has no finite value.

When the distance is negative

Only one form can be negative: the all-demes geometric form (NEI_D_ALL_GEO and NEI_D_ALL_GEO_LOCUS_MEAN). Every pair form and every arithmetic form stays at or above 0.

What is going on

The all-demes geometric identity divides average between-deme sharing by the geometric mean of the within-deme identities. By (N.6), a geometric mean is no larger than the arithmetic mean. If one deme is much more diverse than the others, its within identity is small and pulls the geometric denominator downward. The numerator can then be larger than that denominator, giving I>1. Since ln(I)>0 for I>1, −ln(I) is negative. This can happen only for the all-demes geometric form; pair identities are bounded by one through the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the all-demes arithmetic identity is bounded by one.

A worked example

One locus, three demes:

Deme Allele frequencies Within-deme identity Jk
1 fixed for allele A 1
2 fixed for allele A 1
3 1000 alleles, each at frequency 0.001, including A 0.001

The third deme's within identity is the sum of one thousand equal squared frequencies. For any deme, its own identity is calculated from the squared frequency of each allele:

J3=1000(0.001)2=0.001(N.20)

The between-deme identities are J12=1 (both demes are fixed for A), J13=0.001, and J23=0.001 (A is the only shared allele with deme 3). Average the three unordered pairs:

Jbetween=1+0.001+0.0013=1.0023=0.334(N.21)

The geometric mean of d positive values is their product raised to the power 1/d, as in the denominator of (N.9). For two values, d=2, this is the familiar square root. Here there are three within-deme identities, so d=3 and the exponent is 1/3: taking the cube root. This gives the single value whose product with itself three times equals the original product. Since 0.1·0.1·0.1=0.001, the denominator is:

Jwithin,geo=(1·1·0.001)1/3=0.1(N.22)

Divide the pair average by that denominator, then take the negative logarithm:

Iall,geo=0.3340.1=3.34(N.23)
Dall,geo=−ln(3.34)≈−1.206(N.24)

For comparison, the arithmetic denominator is (1+1+0.001)/3≈0.667. It gives identity approximately 0.501 and distance approximately 0.692, which is positive. The numerator did not change; the choice of within-deme averaging changed the denominator.

How to read it

A negative value does not mean the demes are "closer than identical". It means the question this form asks, "how does typical between-deme sharing compare with the geometric mean of within-deme sharing", is being dominated by a few very diverse demes. Read it as a signal that within-deme diversity is highly unequal, and:

  • look at the arithmetic form beside it, which is not pulled toward the most diverse deme and stays in its ordinary range;
  • look at H_S and the effective number of alleles to see the uneven diversity directly;
  • use pair forms to find which demes drive the difference.

fim reports the value as computed and never clamps it to 0, because clamping would hide exactly this signal. A value within rounding of 0 is shown as 0.

How the forms relate to Jost's D

Let Jwithin be the arithmetic mean of within-deme identities and let Jbetween be the mean between-deme identity. Jost's differentiation statistic (reported under the literal key D) is one minus their ratio:

DJost=1−JbetweenJwithin(N.25)

The all-demes arithmetic Nei identity is that same ratio, and its distance applies the negative logarithm. Therefore:

Iall,arith=1−DJost(N.26)
DNEI,all,arith=−ln(1−DJost)(N.27)

fim's default D combines loci by the same pooled rule (locus_aggregation ratio_of_means), so the relation holds exactly for every report; with mean_of_ratios it holds locus by locus. The same relation, applied to two demes, links each pair arithmetic distance to Jost's D for that pair. It is a useful check: the two are computed by independent code in fim. The equality requires matching locus aggregation and the arithmetic denominator; it does not hold for the geometric forms.

Other measures captured alongside

Computed and saved for every run, hidden until you show them:

  • D_m and R_ST: Nei's (1973) mean pairwise between-deme diversity, in heterozygosity units, and its ratio to within-deme diversity.
  • G_ST_NEI_LOG and G_ST_HEDRICK: two different statistics both called GST′ in the literature. Nei's is logarithmic, for large differentiation; Hedrick's divides GST by its largest possible value.
  • F_ST: coancestry FST (Goudet & Weir 2023).
  • F_ST_PAIR: pairwise FST for the scatter's pair, from those two demes' data alone; every pair is in pairwise.json.
  • Gs and Gd: the within- and between-deme gene identities themselves.

None of these can be chosen as a convergence statistic: the convergence monitor was designed for the long-standing differentiation measures.

A short mathematical refresher

The formulas use only a few familiar operations:

  • Add mutually exclusive cases. At a locus, two copies either both carry allele 1, both carry allele 2, and so on. Those cases cannot happen at once, so their probabilities add. This gives (N.1) within a deme and (N.3) between demes.
  • Multiply independent choices. To match on allele i, the first draw must carry it and the second draw must carry it. Their probabilities multiply, giving pX,ipY,i in (N.3).
  • Compare like quantities. A Nei identity divides a between-deme match rate by a within-deme reference match rate. The numerator is unchanged when choosing the geometric or arithmetic denominator; only the reference changes.
  • Use the logarithm to convert a ratio to a distance. If I=1, then −ln(I)=0. If 0<I<1, then ln(I)<0, so the distance is positive. If I>1, the distance is negative; that case is possible only for the all-demes geometric identity.
  • Keep aggregation order visible. Averaging identities and then taking a ratio is not generally the same as taking one ratio per locus and averaging the resulting distances. Equations (N.11)–(N.19) spell out the two orders rather than hiding them in a formula name.

The worked three-deme example above combines frequency vectors, squared probabilities, pair products, averages, a geometric mean, and the logarithm. Each intermediate value is shown for independent verification.

Formula sheet

For one locus, calculate the within identities with (N.1), the between-deme identities with (N.3), and the all-demes between average with (N.8). Select the geometric or arithmetic denominator with (N.6), (N.9), or (N.10). For multiple loci, use the pooled route in (N.11)–(N.17), or the per-locus route in (N.18) and (N.19). Convert any selected identity to distance with (N.5).

The corresponding result keys use PAIR for a selected pair and ALL for all demes; GEO and ARITH select the denominator. The optional _LOCUS_MEAN suffix selects per-locus distance averaging. For example, NEI_D_PAIR_GEO_LOCUS_MEAN is the geometric-denominator mean of per-locus pair distances, while NEI_D_ALL_ARITH is the pooled all-demes arithmetic-denominator distance.

The identities use fim's with-replacement convention, Jk=∑ipk,i2 (see migration conventions). The statistics reference maps saved identifiers to definitions. The Python API guide and generated API reference document programmatic access. The pair and all-demes calculations are implemented by nei_pair_distance() and nei_all_demes_distance().
